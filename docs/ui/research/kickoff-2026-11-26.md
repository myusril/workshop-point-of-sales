**Evidence tags used throughout**

- `verified` — read directly in a primary or clearly identified public source listed in §Sources, or directly observed in the template's public build.
- `inferred` — reasoned from verified signals; could be wrong.
- `unverified` — hypothesis or assumption with no supporting evidence yet. Treated as a design bet, not a fact.
- `target` — a measurable acceptance threshold proposed for the pilot. It is a design goal, **not** an observed statistic.

## Decision framing

This note exists to change four decisions. If a finding does not touch one of them, it was left out.

| ID | Decision it informs | Why it is expensive to get wrong |
|---|---|---|
| **D1** | Whether the mobile app and the web back-office share one navigation/density model, or split into a task-first cashier shell and a management shell | Determines the information architecture, component library, and how much of the admin template's structure can be reused |
| **D2** | The shape of the core sale: one mixed cart with a persistent summary and a bottom action bar, versus separate product/service screens merged at checkout | Rewrites the primary screen, keyboard behavior, offline queue payload, and receipt layout |
| **D3** | How offline state, sync conflicts, and server-authoritative stock are surfaced to a non-technical cashier | A wrong choice produces silent data loss or duplicated sales that erode trust in the first week |
| **D4** | How much of the admin template's visual language is adopted, and which parts must be original to keep the source handover clean | Affects brand system, component styling, and the license inventory; the template's code and assets cannot be transferred |

**What would change these decisions is stated in §7.2.**

### 1.2 Why this method, and its limits

- Desk research plus heuristic review is the cheapest valid evidence available before buyer access. It can expose structural problems (wrong navigation model, unreadable receipts, unreachable controls) but **cannot** tell us how often they occur in this buyer's workshop.
- **No user access was available.** Nothing in this note is a user quote, a preference claim, or a satisfaction score. Where a claim sounds like user behavior, it is either cited research or explicitly tagged `unverified`.
- The template review covers only the author's public lite build and public demo entry point. The commercial item may differ. No template files are present in the workshop repository at review time, so nothing here should be read as a review of the exact licensed version the buyer may hold.
- The public template build was inspected for layout constants, navigation structure, control sizing, and authentication screens. It was **not** inspected screen-by-screen under load, on a phone, or with assistive technology.

### 1.3 Separation of evidence classes

| Class | Examples in this note | How it may be used |
|---|---|---|
| Verified public evidence | Android share of mobile OS in Indonesia; 58 mm receipts hold 35 monospace columns; WCAG 2.2 minimum target size; capacitive screens need conductive gloves | May be stated as fact with the source |
| Template heuristic findings | Sidebar is 290 px wide and hidden below the `lg` breakpoint; mobile navigation lives in a 330 px drawer; default control height 48 px, extra-small 32 px | Framed as observations with a POS consequence, not as proven usability defects |
| Assumptions | Cashier rush at closing; shared devices; intermittent in-building Wi-Fi; 58 mm printer installed | Must be validated before they justify irreversible design work |

## 2. Context of use

### 2.1 Actors and jobs

| Actor | Primary surface | Job to be done | What "done" looks like | Design consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Owner | Web (occasionally phone) | Know what was sold, what is owed, what is left in stock, and who did what | Daily numbers reconcile; can see exceptions without reading every order | Exception-first dashboards, not chart walls |
| Admin | Web | Keep catalog, prices, customers, vehicles, and stock correct | No duplicate vehicles; prices and stock ledger trustworthy | Bulk-safe edit patterns, audit visibility |
| Cashier | Mobile (possibly also web at a counter) | Take payment quickly and correctly, including mixed product + service sales | Customer leaves with a correct receipt; drawer matches at close | Task-first navigation, large numeric entry, offline-safe |
| Mechanic | Mobile | Record what was found and done on the vehicle mid-bay | Status and labor lines are current without walking to the counter | Status change in ≤ 3 taps, works with gloves/wet hands |

`inferred` from MVP scope. These are role definitions, not observed behavior.

**Jobs-to-be-done statements (working hypotheses)**

1. "When a customer arrives with a vehicle, I need to find that vehicle and its history before I promise anything." (`unverified`)
2. "When a job needs both a part and labor, I need one document and one total, not two systems." (`verified` as a product requirement; behavior `unverified`)
3. "When the internet drops, I still need to take money and hand over a receipt." (`unverified` frequency; requirement is in MVP)
4. "When I close the shop, I need the cash count to match the day without re-adding receipts by hand." (`unverified` current practice; `inferred` from the reconciliation requirement)

### 2.2 Physical and environmental constraints

| Constraint | Evidence | Tag | Design consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Noisy environment.** Receipt printers alone are rated around 55 dB at bystander position (S11); a workshop with impact tools is louder. | Vendor spec + `unverified` workshop measurement | `verified` printer spec / `unverified` workshop level | Never rely on sound alone for success or error. Pair every auditory signal with visual + haptic. |
| **Gloves and wet hands.** Capacitive panels require a conductive path; ordinary gloves and water droplets degrade or block input; vendors ship special glove/wet profiles to compensate (S10). | S10 | `verified` mechanism | Primary targets 56 dp+, generous spacing, no precision gestures, no drag-only actions. Confirm with a gloved-hand test. |
| **Glare and poor lighting.** Workshop bays are often open-air or lit by high-bay lamps; phones are used at an angle. | No measurement | `unverified` | High contrast, avoid low-contrast gray-on-gray, avoid relying on subtle elevation. Test outdoors at midday. |
| **Intermittent connectivity.** National internet penetration is high (79.5% in APJII's 2024 survey, n=8,720, margin of error 1.1% — S2), but penetration is not in-building reliability. | S2 + `unverified` site survey | `verified` penetration / `unverified` workshop signal | Offline-first is a UX state, not an error toast. Queue visibility, per-transaction status, and explicit conflict resolution. |
| **Shared devices.** One phone may be used by cashier and mechanics across shifts. | No evidence | `unverified` | Design for fast identity switch (PIN, not email/password), and scope what a signed-in device may see. Validate before building. |
| **Close-of-day rush.** Payments cluster before closing. | No evidence | `unverified` | Optimize the payment path for repeat, low-reading operation; do not require a network round-trip to confirm cash. |
| **Mid-bay updates.** Mechanic updates status with dirty or gloved hands, standing, one-handed. | S3, S4 for grip; workshop specifics `unverified` | `verified` grip research | Bottom-anchored primary actions; status change reachable from the job card, not from a global menu. |
| **Device class mix.** Android is 78.68% of mobile OS share in Indonesia (October 2026, S1), with a long tail of low-RAM devices. | S1 | `verified` share / `inferred` device performance | Budget for low-end Android: no heavy blur/animation, offline queue must survive process death (S13), and layouts must work at 360 dp width. |
| **Receipt paper.** 58 mm paper at Font A holds **35 columns**; 80 mm holds **48 columns** (S11). | S11 | `verified` | Receipt templates must be designed at 35 columns first, not shrunk from desktop. Long part names need a defined wrap/truncate rule. |

### 2.3 Network and device baseline (what we actually know)

- Android dominates mobile OS share in Indonesia at 78.68% versus iOS 21.28% (October 2026, S1). StatCounter measures page views, not installed base, so treat this as directional. `verified` with limitation.
- 79.5% internet penetration, 221.56 million users (APJII 2024, S2). `verified`.
- **NOT VERIFIED:** typical workshop uplink quality, indoor signal, data plan limits, whether the shop has a UPS, and whether devices are personal or shop-owned.
- **NOT VERIFIED:** the actual phone models and Android versions in use, and whether the shop already owns a 58 mm or 80 mm printer.

### 2.4 Language and terminology

UI copy is Bahasa Indonesia. Formatting is not optional: Indonesian locale uses `.` for thousands and `,` for decimals (CLDR `id`, S15). A price shown as `Rp1,234,567` would read as wrong to an Indonesian user.

Candidate glossary — **all entries require buyer confirmation** (`unverified`):

| Concept | Candidate UI term | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Payment receipt | **Struk** | Some shops call the whole document "nota"; mixing the two confuses reprint requests |
| Service order / job card | **Nota servis** / **Pesanan servis** | "Order" is understood, but "nota" may be expected on paper |
| Labor line | **Jasa** / **Servis** | "Servis" can mean the whole visit, not the labor line |
| Spare part | **Spare part** / **Suku cadang** | Both are used; "spare part" is common in speech, "suku cadang" in formal copy |
| Technician | **Mekanik** / **Teknisi** | Role label affects permissions copy |
| Void | **Batalkan** | Must not be confused with "hapus" (delete); the product never deletes |

Recommendation: pick one term per concept, put it in the product glossary, and never use a synonym in the same screen. `inferred`

## 3. End-to-end task flows

Flows are written as step lists with decision points and failure modes. UI copy examples are in Bahasa Indonesia and are proposals, not approved strings.

### Flow 1 — Walk-in mixed product + service sale

**Goal:** one document, one total, correct stock and revenue split. **Actor:** Cashier.

1. Open **Transaksi Baru** from the home screen (one tap, not via a menu).
2. Optional: attach vehicle via **Cari nomor polisi** (Flow 2). If skipped, the sale is marked **Tanpa kendaraan** and cannot appear in vehicle history.
3. Add a part: scan barcode → quantity keypad → line appears with **Spare part** badge.
4. Add labor: search **Jasa servis** → pick service → line appears with **Jasa** badge and estimated duration.
5. Review the cart: subtotal per category, discount, tax, total.
6. Apply discount: amount or percent, with reason if above the cashier's limit.
7. **Bayar** → payment sheet (Flow 4).
8. Receipt preview → **Cetak struk** and/or **Bagikan struk**.
9. Order status moves to **Sudah dibayar**; stock decrements per the inventory rule.

**Decision points / failure modes**

- A part and a service can share a name ("Oli" vs "Ganti oli"). Category must be visible on every line, not only in the total. `inferred`
- Editing a price must be permission-gated and leave an audit entry; otherwise revenue reporting is untrustworthy. `inferred`
- If the sale is created offline, the receipt must show a clear local marker and the queue state (Flow 8).
- Cash rounding rules are unknown (`unverified`); the UI must not silently invent rounding.

**UI copy examples:** `Transaksi Baru` · `Tambah spare part` · `Tambah jasa` · `Subtotal spare part` · `Subtotal jasa` · `Diskon` · `Pajak` · `Total` · `Bayar` · `Tanpa kendaraan`

### Flow 2 — Service intake with vehicle and plate capture

**Goal:** a vehicle exists exactly once, and its history is findable later. **Actor:** Cashier (service desk); Admin for corrections.

1. **Cari nomor polisi** is the first field on the intake screen and on the home screen.
2. Type any format: `B 1234 ABC`, `b1234abc`, `B-1234-ABC`. Input is uppercased and separators are stripped as the user types.
3. Results show plate in canonical display form, brand/model, owner name, and **Servis terakhir** date + odometer.
4. If no match: **Kendaraan belum terdaftar** → **Tambah kendaraan**.
5. Vehicle form: plate (prefilled from search), brand, model, year, color, odometer, notes. Customer: name, phone (optional), notes.
6. Save. A duplicate check runs on the normalized plate before insert.
7. Start the order: complaint (**Keluhan pelanggan**), initial odometer, assigned **Mekanik**.
8. Print or share an intake slip with queue number and promised time.

**Decision points / failure modes**

- Plate structure in Indonesia is region letters + serial digits + 2–3 suffix letters, with special suffixes for government/diplomatic vehicles (S22). `verified`
- **Normalization is not just removing spaces.** Serial digit length varies and users type `-`, `.`, or nothing. Store one canonical key (uppercase alphanumeric only) and display a readable form. `inferred`
- Do **not** auto-pad zeros to a fixed width unless the buyer confirms their Samsat documents do so. Padding could merge two different vehicles. `inferred`
- If the plate exists under a different owner (resale), the vehicle record must be transferable without losing history. Ownership model is an open question (§8). `unverified`
- OCR plate capture is an accelerator, never the only path (see §5.4).

**UI copy examples:** `Cari nomor polisi` · `Kendaraan belum terdaftar` · `Tambah kendaraan` · `Nomor polisi` · `Merek` · `Model` · `Tahun` · `Warna` · `Odometer (km)` · `Nama pelanggan` · `Nomor HP (opsional)` · `Keluhan pelanggan` · `Mekanik` · `Mulai pengerjaan`

### Flow 3 — Estimate approval

**Goal:** a documented approval before work exceeds what the customer agreed to. **Actor:** Cashier (service desk); approver is the customer.

1. Add parts and labor as in Flow 1, but choose **Simpan sebagai estimasi**.
2. Status: **Draft** → **Estimasi dikirim**.
3. Send: print, share as PDF via the system share sheet, or show on screen.
4. Record the approval: who approved, channel (**Langsung**, **WhatsApp**, **Telepon**), timestamp, optional note. Status → **Disetujui**. Or → **Ditolak** with reason.
5. On approval, the order becomes **Dikerjakan**.
6. Any change after approval shows a diff: **Perubahan estimasi: +Rp250.000** and requires re-approval before work continues.

**Decision points / failure modes**

- Approval is operational evidence, not a legal determination. The product should record what happened and who recorded it, and leave legal sufficiency to the buyer. `inferred`
- A verbal "yes" is common; the UI must make the channel explicit so nobody later claims written approval exists. `unverified` practice
- Re-approval after a price change is the main protection against dispute; make the diff impossible to miss. `inferred`
- If the customer approves by WhatsApp, the app cannot verify the sender. Label it **Disetujui (dicatat manual)** rather than implying verification. `inferred`

**UI copy examples:** `Simpan sebagai estimasi` · `Kirim estimasi` · `Menunggu persetujuan` · `Disetujui` · `Ditolak` · `Disetujui oleh` · `Melalui` · `Perubahan estimasi` · `Perlu persetujuan ulang`

### Flow 4 — Split payment and receipt

**Goal:** record a sale paid with more than one method, with a receipt the customer accepts. **Actor:** Cashier.

1. **Bayar** opens the payment sheet with the outstanding total.
2. Choose method: **Tunai**, **Transfer bank**, **QRIS** (recorded manually in MVP).
3. Enter amount: custom numeric keypad with `000` and backspace, live formatted to `Rp1.234.567`.
4. If amount < total: **Bayar sebagian** → remaining shown as **Sisa tagihan**, method list stays open.
5. Repeat for the next method. Non-cash methods capture a reference: bank name, last digits, approval code, or QRIS reference. Mark **Belum terverifikasi** until reconciled (Flow 9).
6. When outstanding reaches zero: for cash, show **Uang diterima** and **Kembalian**; for mixed payments, cash change is calculated from the cash portion only.
7. Confirm → receipt preview → **Cetak struk**, **Bagikan struk**, or **Simpan PDF**.
8. Receipt shows each tender line, not just "paid".

**Decision points / failure modes**

- QRIS is a national QR payment standard regulated by Bank Indonesia (S21). In MVP the app records the reference, not the money movement, so the UI must never imply the payment was verified. `verified` standard / `inferred` wording
- Overpayment beyond the total on non-cash methods must be blocked, not silently accepted. `inferred`
- A split payment created offline must keep the same idempotency key so retries do not duplicate tender lines. `inferred`
- 58 mm receipts hold 35 columns (S11); a tender table with method + amount + reference will overflow. Use two lines per tender: method and amount on one, reference on the next.

**UI copy examples:** `Metode pembayaran` · `Tunai` · `Transfer bank` · `QRIS` · `Nomor referensi` · `Bayar sebagian` · `Sisa tagihan` · `Uang diterima` · `Kembalian` · `Belum terverifikasi` · `Cetak struk` · `Bagikan struk`

### Flow 5 — Void and correction

**Goal:** fix mistakes without destroying the audit trail. **Actor:** Cashier for drafts; Admin/Owner for paid orders.

1. Open the order → **Batalkan transaksi**.
2. If unpaid: choose a reason from a list plus optional note. Order becomes **Dibatalkan**; stock reservations release.
3. If paid: **Batalkan & kembalikan dana** requires a manager PIN, a reason, and a refund method. A reversal entry is created; the original receipt number is never reused.
4. Receipt reprint is marked **DIBATALKAN** and shows the original timestamp.
5. Stock returns only for parts not yet consumed.
6. Audit log records actor, role, reason, timestamp, before/after amounts.

**Decision points / failure modes**

- "Void" and "delete" must never be the same action in the UI. The word **Hapus** should not appear on financial records. `inferred`
- Corrections before completion should be an edit, not a void + recreate; otherwise vehicle history fills with noise. `inferred`
- Refund method may differ from payment method (cash refund for a QRIS payment). The UI must allow it and record both. `unverified` practice
- A reprint counter prevents a receipt being used twice; at minimum, reprints must be distinguishable from originals. `inferred`

**UI copy examples:** `Batalkan transaksi` · `Alasan pembatalan` · `Batalkan & kembalikan dana` · `Dana dikembalikan melalui` · `Dibatalkan oleh` · `Cetak ulang` · `Salinan`

### Flow 6 — Vehicle history lookup

**Goal:** answer "what did we do to this vehicle last time?" in seconds. **Actor:** Anyone at the counter; Mechanic.

1. Global search field, first item on the home screen: **Cari nomor polisi, nama pelanggan, atau nomor nota**.
2. Plate query uses the same normalization as intake; a suffix-only query (`ABC`) should still find candidates.
3. Result card: vehicle identity, owner, last odometer, **Servis terakhir**, total visits.
4. Open **Riwayat kendaraan**: reverse-chronological list with date, order type, items, labor, invoice total, odometer, mechanic.
5. Tap an entry → full order, receipt, and status history.
6. Actions: **Cetak ulang**, **Buat pesanan baru dari kendaraan ini** (prefills vehicle).

**Decision points / failure modes**

- Ambiguous plates (same suffix, different region) must show region and owner, not just the plate. `inferred`
- If the buyer operates multiple outlets, history scope is an open question (§8). `unverified`
- Odometer entries must show whether they came from intake, mechanic, or invoice; otherwise the trend is meaningless. `inferred`
- Phone number should be masked in list views by default (see §8.2). `inferred`

**UI copy examples:** `Riwayat kendaraan` · `Servis terakhir` · `Kunjungan` · `Odometer saat masuk` · `Dikerjakan oleh` · `Buat pesanan baru`

### Flow 7 — Stock adjustment

**Goal:** change stock with a reason and a paper trail. **Actor:** Admin; Cashier may request.

1. Inventory list → search or scan → item detail.
2. **Sesuaikan stok** (never a directly editable quantity field).
3. Choose movement type: **Barang masuk**, **Retur**, **Rusak/hilang**, **Koreksi hitung**, **Pemakaian internal**.
4. Enter quantity and reason; optional reference (supplier note, count sheet).
5. Preview: **Stok saat ini 12 → Stok setelah 9**, with the delta in red/green plus text.
6. Save → ledger entry with actor and timestamp. On-hand is derived, not overwritten.
7. Low-stock list updates; reorder threshold is per item.

**Decision points / failure modes**

- A ledger-first model is the only way to explain a discrepancy later. Direct quantity edits destroy that. `inferred`
- Negative stock must be a deliberate policy, not an accident of offline sync (Flow 8). `inferred`
- Cashiers should not silently adjust stock to make a sale pass. Requests need approval or a threshold. `inferred`
- Count corrections should support "counted quantity" entry, not "difference" arithmetic, because counting is what the user actually did. `inferred`

**UI copy examples:** `Sesuaikan stok` · `Jenis penyesuaian` · `Barang masuk` · `Koreksi hitung` · `Rusak/hilang` · `Stok saat ini` · `Stok setelah` · `Alasan` · `Jumlah terhitung`

### Flow 8 — Offline sale, then sync

**Goal:** never lose a sale, never silently duplicate one, never hide a conflict. **Actor:** Cashier.

1. Connectivity lost → persistent status chip in the app bar: **Offline · 3 transaksi menunggu**.
2. Sale proceeds normally. Local order gets a device-scoped ID and an idempotency key.
3. Receipt prints or shares with **Salinan lokal — belum tersinkron** and a human-readable local reference.
4. Queue screen lists pending items with state: **Menunggu**, **Mengirim**, **Gagal**, **Perlu tinjauan**.
5. On reconnect, WorkManager-style persistent work drains the queue (S13); retries use exponential backoff.
6. Server assigns the final invoice number. Receipt reprint shows the final number.
7. If stock is insufficient at sync, the order is **kept** and flagged **Perlu tinjauan** with the delta. It is never deleted.
8. Conflict inbox groups all exceptions for one person to resolve: accept a negative-stock record with approval, correct stock, or void with reason.

**Decision points / failure modes**

- Silent conflict resolution is the worst outcome. A cashier who discovers a vanished sale stops trusting the app. `inferred`
- Cash is offline-safe; QRIS and transfer are not verifiable offline. Their receipts must say **Belum terverifikasi** until Flow 9. `inferred`
- Device clock skew will happen; reports must use server time, while the UI may show device time for the human record. `inferred`
- Two phones can sell the last unit. The server must be authoritative and the UI must explain the outcome in shop language, not HTTP codes. `inferred`
- The queue must survive app kill and reboot; that is a platform capability, not a UI promise (S13). `verified`

**UI copy examples:** `Offline` · `Transaksi menunggu` · `Sinkronkan sekarang` · `Tersinkron` · `Gagal sinkron` · `Perlu tinjauan` · `Stok tidak cukup` · `Salinan lokal` · `Belum tersinkron`

### Flow 9 — End-of-day reconciliation

**Goal:** close the day with numbers that match reality. **Actor:** Cashier closes the shift; Owner closes the day.

1. **Tutup kasir** shows a summary before anything is locked: total sales, cash, transfer, QRIS, discounts, voids, pending sync.
2. Cash section: **Uang awal**, **Penjualan tunai**, **Pengembalian tunai**, **Uang seharusnya**, then **Uang fisik dihitung**.
3. Variance: **Selisih kas** in rupiah; if non-zero, a reason is required.
4. Non-cash section: each tender line with reference and verification state; mark **Terverifikasi**.
5. Pending offline transactions are listed with a hard stop: closing is blocked, or requires an explicit override with a reason.
6. **Tutup hari** (Owner/Admin) produces the closing summary (the Z report), locks the day, and exports CSV.
7. Summary is printable and shareable; audit log records who closed and any override.

**Decision points / failure modes**

- Two different closes (shift vs day) prevent the common failure where one person's close hides another's pending work. `inferred`
- A close that silently ignores pending sync is worse than a blocked close. `inferred`
- Variance must be shown as an absolute rupiah amount, not a percentage, because cash is counted in rupiah. `inferred`
- Owner needs the exception list (voids, discounts, negative stock, overrides), not just totals. `inferred`

**UI copy examples:** `Tutup kasir` · `Tutup hari` · `Uang awal` · `Uang seharusnya` · `Uang fisik dihitung` · `Selisih kas` · `Transaksi tertunda` · `Tutup tetap` · `Alasan` · `Ekspor CSV`

## 4. Heuristic findings: the referenced admin template

### 4.1 What was reviewed, and the limits

Reviewed artifacts: the referenced Ecme admin template (Next.js) via its author's public lite build and public demo entry point (S18). The commercial item itself is licensed per end product (S17) and is not part of the workshop repository at review time.

**Limits:** no task-based usability test, no assistive-technology pass, no mobile-device pass, and no access to the full item's page library. The public lite build exposes no table, chart, card, or modal-dialog component in its published component set, so findings about table density, card hierarchy, and modal depth are partly genre-level inference and are tagged as such. Conclusions below are structural observations with POS consequences, not measured defect counts.

Method: heuristic evaluation against Nielsen's ten heuristics (S19), with a POS-specific lens: time pressure, gloved hands, one-handed reach, mixed-cart editing, offline states, and money handling.

### 4.2 Strengths worth keeping (as patterns, not as copied files)

| Observation | Tag | Why it helps a POS product |
|---|---|---|
| Six layout modes are defined (collapsible side, stacked side, top bar, frameless, content overlay, blank) | `verified` | Lets the web back-office and a counter/tablet view diverge without forking the design system |
| A consistent control-size scale exists (extra-small 32 px, small 40 px, default 48 px, large 56 px) | `verified` | A single token scale makes the "48 dp minimum, 56 dp for gloves" rule enforceable instead of ad hoc |
| Page composition is a consistent page header (title plus optional action area) above a full-height content region | `verified` | Predictable orientation for back-office scanning: where am I, what can I do here |
| The item is positioned as a general-purpose admin dashboard with reusable components and pre-designed pages (S18); table and chart screens are expected in the full item | `inferred` from the public description; not observed in the lite build | Density suits back-office lists and reports, not the cashier's transaction screen |
| Light/dark theming and an RTL direction switch are built in | `verified` | Proves the token layer can be re-themed for the buyer's brand without template assets |
| Authentication screens exist for sign-in, forgot/reset password, and social sign-in | `verified` | Useful for Owner/Admin web access; not sufficient for shop-floor identities |
| Navigation supports groups and nested collapse items | `verified` | Adequate for a management console with many modules; a liability for the cashier shell (see 4.3) |

### 4.3 Risks for POS use

| Finding | Evidence | Severity | POS consequence | Recommended response |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## 5. Mobile-specific findings

### 5.1 Grip, reach, and where actions belong

Hoober's field research reports that 75% of users touch the screen only with a thumb, fewer than 50% hold the phone with one hand, 36% cradle it, and 10% hold it in one hand and tap with the other (S4). Earlier observation data found 49% one-handed, 36% cradled, 15% two-handed among 780 screen-touching observations (S3), with the caveat that the 2017 article supersedes parts of the 2013 guidance.

Practical implications, all `inferred`:

- Put the primary action (**Bayar**, **Simpan**) in a fixed bottom bar, not a top-right button.
- Put destructive actions (**Batalkan**) behind a deliberate step, away from the primary action; never adjacent to it.
- Keep the center of the screen for the work (cart lines, job card), matching Hoober's finding that people look and tap at the center.
- Support both hands: do not assume a right thumb. Mirror nothing, but avoid edge-anchored critical controls.

Hoober also reports that accuracy is highest at screen center (targets can be as small as 7 mm) and lowest at corners (about 12 mm needed) (S4). Design rule: corners are for low-frequency or destructive actions, never for the primary commit.

### 5.2 Touch targets, gloves, and wet hands

- Android recommends at least 48×48 dp for interactive elements (S5); WCAG 2.2 sets a 24×24 CSS px minimum with spacing exceptions (S6). The 24 px floor is a conformance floor, not a shop-floor standard. `verified`
- Ordinary gloves often block or degrade capacitive input; water on the screen causes false or missed touches; vendors provide glove/wet profiles to compensate (S10). `verified` mechanism
- **Recommended:** 56 dp minimum for primary controls, 48 dp for secondary, 8 dp minimum spacing, no long-press-only actions. `inferred`
- **Recommended:** avoid swipe-to-delete and drag-to-reorder in the cart; use visible +/− and explicit remove. This also satisfies WCAG 2.5.7 Dragging Movements (S7). `inferred`

### 5.3 Numeric entry

- Cash and quantity entry dominate the session. Use an in-app numeric keypad for amounts: large keys, `000`, backspace, and a live formatted value. Do not depend on the system keyboard's layout or autocorrect. `inferred`
- Show the amount twice: as typed digits and as formatted rupiah, so a missing zero is visible before commit. `inferred`
- Use integer minor units internally; never show decimals for IDR. `verified` product constraint
- Decimal separator must be `,` and grouping `.` per Indonesian locale (S15). `verified`
- Validate on blur, not on every keystroke; error text in Bahasa Indonesia, next to the field, not in a toast. `inferred`

### 5.4 Plate capture and search

- One input, not three (region / number / suffix). Auto-uppercase, strip separators, show the canonical display form below the field. `inferred`
- Search should tolerate suffix-only queries because mechanics remember the letters, not the digits. `inferred`
- Camera OCR is a valid accelerator: ML Kit's Latin text recognition runs on-device and is real-time on most devices; the bundled model adds about 4 MB per script/architecture and works without a model download, while the unbundled variant depends on Google Play services (S14). `verified`
- OCR must always produce an editable field, with the captured plate shown as a suggestion. A misread plate that is accepted silently corrupts history. `inferred`
- **NOT VERIFIED:** OCR accuracy on Indonesian plates in workshop lighting, with dirt, frames, or non-standard fonts. Do not promise scan-first intake before testing.

### 5.5 Offline state visibility

- Persistent, always-visible status: icon + text + count. Color alone fails WCAG 1.4.1 Use of Color (S23) and is unreadable in glare. `inferred`
- Per-transaction state badges in lists and on the receipt preview. `inferred`
- A queue screen with retry, not a transient toast. `inferred`
- Conflicts get their own inbox with a plain-language explanation and a decision, not an automatic fix. `inferred`
- Background sync must be implemented as persistent work that survives app exit and reboot (S13). `verified` capability

### 5.6 Receipt printing and share fallbacks

- 58 mm paper: 35 columns at Font A; 80 mm: 48 columns; print width 52.5 mm and 72 mm respectively (S11). Design at 35 columns first. `verified`
- Layout rule: item name on one line (wrap to a second line if needed), quantity × unit price right-aligned, amount on its own line if the name is long. `inferred`
- Always provide a non-thermal fallback: generate a PDF and open the Android Sharesheet, which is the platform-standard way to hand content to another app (S12). `verified` capability
- Reprint must be labeled **Salinan** and increment a reprint count in the audit log. `inferred`
- **NOT VERIFIED:** which printer models, connection types (Bluetooth/USB/network), and paper width the buyer actually uses.

### 5.7 Feedback and error handling

- Never rely on sound in a noisy bay; pair visual state with haptic confirmation. Epson rates its receipt printer at about 55 dB at bystander position (S11), before tools are running. `verified` printer noise / `inferred` workshop noise
- Response-time targets from Nielsen: 0.1 s feels instantaneous, 1 s keeps flow, 10 s is the attention limit and needs progress feedback (S20). `verified` guideline
- For POS, the commit action (payment) must acknowledge locally within 1 s even when the network is slow; the sync happens afterward. `inferred`
- Errors must say what happened and what to do next, in shop language: **Stok tidak cukup. Kurangi jumlah atau lanjutkan sebagai pesanan khusus.** `inferred`

## 6. Accessibility risks and recommended checks

Target: WCAG 2.2 AA on web; Android accessibility guidance on mobile. WCAG 2.2 adds nine criteria over 2.1, including Focus Not Obscured, Dragging Movements, Consistent Help, Redundant Entry, and Accessible Authentication (S7).

### 6.1 Risk register

| Risk | Why it is likely here | Criterion / guidance | Check | Pass threshold (`target`) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low-contrast secondary text in dense tables and status chips | Admin templates often use gray-on-gray for meta text | 1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum), 4.5:1 normal text, 3:1 large (S8) | Automated contrast scan + manual spot check on every token pair | 0 text pairs below 4.5:1 (3:1 for ≥ 24 px or ≥ 18.66 px bold) |
| Undersized icon buttons (edit, print, delete, more) | Icon-only actions in tables are typically 32 px or smaller | 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum), 24×24 CSS px (S6); Android 48 dp (S5) | Measure every interactive element's bounding box on 360 dp and 1280 px | Web ≥ 24 px and ≥ 44 px for primary; mobile ≥ 48 dp |
| Cart editing requires drag or precise swipe | Common pattern for reordering or deleting lines | 2.5.7 Dragging Movements (S7) | Attempt all cart operations with keyboard and single taps | 100% of cart operations achievable without dragging |
| Focus lost behind sticky bottom bars and sheets | Fixed payment bars obscure focused fields | 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (Minimum) (S7) | Keyboard-only pass with 200% zoom | Focused element never fully hidden |
| Dialog and drawer focus management | Drawer, dropdown, and tooltip overlays are built into the template's component set | 2.4.3 Focus Order and 2.1.2 No Keyboard Trap, both Level A and therefore required under AA conformance (S23) | Keyboard-only traversal of intake → estimate → payment | Focus trapped only inside an open modal, returned on close, order logical |
| Re-asking customer/vehicle data across steps | Intake, estimate, and payment are separate screens | 3.3.7 Redundant Entry (S7) | Walk the flow and record every repeated field | 0 repeated fields except confirmation of the plate |
| Login requires typing a password | Shared shop-floor device | 3.3.8 Accessible Authentication (S7) | Attempt sign-in with a PIN and with a password manager | PIN or device credential accepted; no puzzle CAPTCHA |
| Icon-only buttons without names | Print, share, more, delete are icon-only by convention | 4.1.2 Name, Role, Value (S23) | TalkBack/VoiceOver pass on key screens | Every control announces a meaningful label in Bahasa Indonesia |
| Status conveyed by color only | Offline, paid, void, low stock are natural color codes | 1.4.1 Use of Color (S23) | Grayscale screenshot review | Every status has text or icon in addition to color |
| Horizontal scrolling at 320 px / 400% zoom | Data tables with many columns | 1.4.10 Reflow (S9) | Browser at 320 CSS px and 400% zoom | Page scrolls in one direction; tables may scroll inside their own container |
| Receipt unreadable at 58 mm | 35 columns, small thermal font (S11) | Not a WCAG surface, but a real accessibility issue | Print 20 worst-case receipts at 58 mm and 80 mm | 0 truncations; plate, totals, and payment lines read back correctly by 3 staff at arm's length; key totals bold |

### 6.2 Recommended check protocol (desktop web)

1. **Automated:** axe-core (or equivalent) on 10 key screens: sign-in, home, new sale, intake, estimate, payment, receipt, inventory list, stock adjust, close-day.
2. **Keyboard-only:** complete one mixed sale, one void, and one stock adjustment without a mouse.
3. **Zoom and reflow:** 200% text, 400% zoom at 1280 px, and a 320 CSS px viewport.
4. **Screen reader:** one pass with a desktop screen reader on the sale and close-day screens, checking table headers, live totals, and dialog announcements.
5. **Contrast:** token-level audit of text, icons, borders, focus rings, and disabled states.

### 6.3 Recommended check protocol (Android)

1. TalkBack pass on home, intake, payment, queue, and receipt preview.
2. Switch Access or keyboard pass for the same screens.
3. Font scale at maximum and display size at maximum; verify no clipped totals.
4. Touch-target measurement at 360 dp width.
5. Gloved-hand and wet-hand test on the actual device class (see §8.4).

## 7. Top 10 prioritized UX recommendations

Priority reflects risk to money, trust, and handover. Acceptance signals are **targets**, not observed results.

| # | Recommendation | Rationale | Evidence | Measurable acceptance signal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Split the product into two shells:** a task-first cashier app (bottom tabs: **Jual**, **Pesanan**, **Stok**, **Lainnya**) and a management back-office with the admin language | The template's desktop shell and nested navigation are poorly suited to a 360 dp phone under time pressure | S18 (sidebar 290 px, drawer 330 px, nested menus), S4 (thumb-first use) | Cashier reaches any core task in ≤ 1 navigation level; ≥ 90% of pilot participants complete a mixed sale without opening a menu |
| **2** | **One persistent mixed cart** with visible `Spare part` / `Jasa` category on each line, category subtotals, and a fixed bottom total bar | Mixing products and services is the core product promise; ambiguity here corrupts both revenue reporting and receipts | MVP scope; S11 (receipt width) | 100% of pilot test carts show category subtotals before payment; 0 misclassified lines in a 50-line audit |
| **3** | **Plate-first global search** with canonical normalization, suffix matching, and a duplicate guard before vehicle creation | Vehicle history is worthless if the same vehicle exists twice; plate is the natural key | S22 (plate structure), S15 (locale) | ≥ 95% first-attempt lookup success over 100 pilot lookups; duplicate-vehicle creation rate < 1% in the first month |
| **4** | **Estimate approval as an explicit state** with approver, channel, timestamp, and a visible diff on any post-approval change | Prevents disputes and makes "who agreed to what" answerable | S19 (visibility of system status) | 100% of estimates above a configurable threshold have an approval record; 0 orders that changed price without a re-approval prompt in tests |
| **5** | **Split payment as a first-class sheet** with per-tender reference capture, "remaining" always visible, and cash change from the cash portion only | Mixed payments are common at closing; wrong change is an immediate trust failure | S21 (QRIS standard), S11 | 0 arithmetic errors across 200 generated split-payment cases; cashier completes a 2-method payment in ≤ 6 interactions |
| **6** | **Offline as a visible state machine**, with a queue screen, per-transaction badges, and a conflict inbox that never deletes a sale | Silent loss or duplication destroys adoption faster than any missing feature | S13 (persistent work), S19 | 0 silent losses in a 2-hour airplane-mode test with 30 queued sales; every conflict appears in the inbox within one sync cycle |
| **7** | **Void and correction as reversals**, never deletion, with reason + actor + refund method, and reprints marked `Salinan` / `DIBATALKAN` | Financial records must remain explainable for audit and handover | S19 (user control, error recovery) | 100% of voided orders reconcile to zero in the ledger; 100% carry actor, reason, timestamp; 0 reused invoice numbers |
| **8** | **Stock adjustments only through a typed movement** showing `Stok saat ini → Stok setelah`, with reason codes and approval thresholds | Direct quantity edits make shrinkage and counting errors unexplainable | S19 | 0 UI paths that write on-hand directly; 100% of adjustments have a reason and actor in tests |
| **9** | **Two-stage close** (`Tutup kasir`, then `Tutup hari`) with cash variance in rupiah and a hard stop on pending sync | Closing is the moment trust is won or lost; hiding pending work makes tomorrow's numbers wrong | S19 | Cash variance shown to the rupiah in 100% of closes; closing blocked or explicitly overridden with a recorded reason whenever pending > 0 |
| **10** | **Accessibility and touch baseline enforced in CI and design review:** 48 dp mobile targets (56 dp primary), 24 px web floor (44 px for primary web controls), 4.5:1 contrast, visible focus, no drag-only actions | WCAG 2.2 AA is a stated non-functional requirement and a handover quality signal | S5, S6, S7, S8, S9 | 0 critical/serious automated violations on 10 key screens; 100% of primary mobile actions ≥ 56 dp; keyboard-only completion of 3 admin tasks |

### 7.1 The three decisions to make first

1. **D1 — two shells, not one responsive admin.** Highest leverage; it prevents six months of retrofitting a desktop navigation model onto a phone. Evidence: template structure (S18) plus grip research (S4).
2. **D2 — persistent mixed cart with category subtotals.** It defines the primary screen, the receipt, and the offline payload. Evidence: MVP requirement plus 35-column receipt constraint (S11).
3. **D3 — offline visibility with an explicit conflict inbox.** It defines trust. Evidence: platform persistence (S13) and visibility heuristic (S19).

### 7.2 What would falsify these recommendations

| Recommendation | Falsified if |
|---|---|
| Two shells | Pilot cashiers using the web back-office on a tablet are consistently faster than the mobile app, and no task requires one-handed use |
| Persistent mixed cart | Cashiers reliably complete mixed sales faster when parts and labor are entered on separate screens and merged at payment |
| Plate-first lookup | Real intake data shows most vehicles are new (no history to look up) and customers are identified by name/phone instead |
| Estimate approval state | The buyer never issues estimates above a threshold, or approvals are always captured on paper and never disputed |
| Split payment sheet | Pilot data shows < 5% of sales use more than one method (then simplify, but keep the data model) |
| Offline conflict inbox | The shop has stable power and connectivity and never sells offline (then deprioritize, but keep the idempotency key) |
| Void as reversal | Not a hypothesis: deletion would break the stated audit requirement, so treat it as a constraint |
| Ledger-only stock | The buyer never needs to explain a discrepancy and accepts that a single edited quantity is auditable — still weaker for reconciliation |
| Two-stage close | The shop has one shift and one cashier, where a single close is sufficient — simplify the labels, keep the variance math |
| Accessibility baseline | Never falsifiable; it is a requirement. Only the target sizes are negotiable within WCAG limits |

## 9. What was not verified, and the cheapest next validation

### 9.1 Not verified

- Any behavior of real cashiers, mechanics, or owners. No interviews, no observation, no task timings. `unverified`
- Workshop connectivity, noise, lighting, and device mix. `unverified`
- Whether gloves are worn at the counter, at the bay, or both. `unverified`
- Printer models, paper width, and printing reliability. `unverified`
- OCR accuracy on real Indonesian plates in workshop conditions. `unverified`
- The buyer's tax, rounding, approval, and retention rules. `unverified`
- The exact licensed template version and its full page library. `unverified`

### 9.2 Cheapest next validation (5 working days, no code)

1. **Two observation sessions** at a live workshop: one at opening/intake, one at closing. Record task steps, interruptions, and where the phone is put down. No names, no customer data; use anonymized IDs and aggregate counts only.
2. **One artifact review:** a redacted current nota/estimate and the shop's closing tally sheet. This answers Q10, Q12, and Q24 faster than interviews.
3. **One device and printer audit:** photograph the phone model, Android version, printer label, and paper width; run a 10-receipt print test with the longest product names.
4. **One gloved/wet-hand tap test:** 20 taps per condition on the target phone, measuring misses; this converts an assumption into a design constraint.
5. **One 30-minute walkthrough** of the nine flows in §3 with the person who will actually use the app, using paper or a clickable prototype. Capture where they hesitate; do not ask for opinions about colors.

### 9.3 Privacy and consent escalations for the founder

1. **Controller identity and notice.** After handover the buyer is the data controller. The product needs a privacy notice and a collection point for customer name/phone/plate. The founder/buyer must supply the legal text; this note does not provide legal advice. `verified` legal frame (S16), `unverified` buyer policy.
2. **Data minimization.** Phone and VIN should be optional unless operationally required. Free-text notes must warn against entering ID numbers or other sensitive data. `inferred`
3. **Sensitive data.** UU PDP distinguishes specific (including financial) and general personal data, and covers data combined to identify a person (S16). Payment references, plate + owner, and service history fall into scope; they need retention and access rules, not just storage. `verified` legal frame (S16)
4. **Logging hygiene.** Do not log plate numbers, phone numbers, or customer names in application logs, error trackers, or analytics. Redact at the source. `inferred`
5. **Exports.** CSV export of sales and vehicle history is a personal-data transfer. It must be role-gated, logged, and limited to the fields needed. `inferred`
6. **Offline risk.** A lost phone contains customer data. Require encrypted local storage, PIN lock, remote revoke, and a documented wipe procedure. `inferred`
7. **Third parties.** Sharing a receipt through the Android Sharesheet is user-initiated. Any future automatic WhatsApp/SMS sending is a third-party processor decision requiring a DPA review before launch. `inferred`

### 9.4 Licensing escalation

The referenced template is a commercial item under Envato's Standard License: one end product, no use in stock items/templates, and no reuse across multiple clients or jobs (S17). For an outright source-code handover, its code and assets are **non-transferable**. The visual language may be approximated only through an original clean-room implementation. This must appear in the license inventory and be confirmed by the founder before any designer opens the template for reference.
